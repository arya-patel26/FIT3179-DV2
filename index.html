Australian Taxation by Region

Discover insights into taxation patterns across Australian communities, by comparing taxpayer numbers, taxable income and demogrpahics of taxpayers.

While the majority of Australian regions maintain median incomes within a consistent baseline range, high-earning population pockets emerge in regional Western Australia and Queensland. This geographical concentration highlights the significant impact of high-paying roles within the mining and resources sectors on regional average earnings.

All Australian states experienced robust growth in tax revenue over time, with New South Wales, Victoria, and Queensland leading total contributions. Notably, Victoria and Queensland recorded the highest rate of tax revenue growth, whereas the Northern Territory recorded the slowest expansion.

Healthcare and finance generated the highest average individual tax contributions across all industry sectors. Mining and resources also ranked among the top five, underscoring the strength of Australia’s primary industry, while logistics and hospitality recorded the lowest average contributions.

Most tax streams maintained steady annual growth, though overall tax revenue experienced a sharp peak following the 2019–2020 financial year. This spike reflects post-pandemic fiscal adjustments, shifts in tax policies, and the rollout of government stimulus and emergency aid programs.

Diesel and petrol consistently held the top spots as major sources of excise tax revenue, whereas cigarettes saw a dramatic fall in ranking before disappearing, alongside tobacco which only featured in the initial dataset. Conversely, spirits demonstrated a notable upward trajectory, significantly increasing their proportional revenue contribution over time.

The top-ranking high-income postcodes are heavily concentrated in major metropolitan hubs, with seven located in New South Wales, two in Victoria, and one in Western Australia. A notable exception is postcode 3944 (Portsea), which recently surged to the top rank due to a small, high-net-worth resident population distorting the average earnings.

Comparing taxpayer counts to net tax contributions reveals a growing revenue concentration, with the $180,000+ tax bracket expanding its share of net tax from 26% to 39%. The $45,000–$120,000 bracket represents the second-largest contribution at 37%, illustrating how inflation, bracket creep, and shifting income distributions have reshaped tax shares over time.
