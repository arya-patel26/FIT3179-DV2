Australian Taxation & Income Trends

Behind every dollar of public revenue sits a decision, an industry and a household. In 2023-24, Australia collected approximately $631 billion in tax, more than double the revenue raised in 2009-10. Individual income tax accounts for just over half of that total, company tax for close to a quarter, and the remainder is drawn from the GST, superannuation funds, excise and other levies.

This dashboard traces that revenue from the national ledger down to the individual taxpayer. It examines how the burden is distributed across states and postcodes, the role of taxes beyond income, and the people and industries that ultimately carry it.

BEYOND INCOME TAX

The other levers

Excise on fuel and alcohol, gains on assets, and generosity claimed at tax time each tell a distinct fiscal story.

Fuel dominates excise while tobacco fades

Top Excise Tax Contributors

Fuel first

Diesel and petrol

Diesel and petrol hold the top spots. Spirits climbed steadily, while cigarettes and tobacco dropped out of the ranking entirely.

Capital gains tax is unevenly spread across entities and assets

Capital Gains Tax Breakdown by Entity and Asset Type

48%

Of gains sit in super funds

Capital gains are heavily skewed: super funds hold the most, led by other assets and shares. Individuals follow at about 40%, mostly in real estate, so wealth sits with individuals and their super funds rather than companies.

Giving varies noticeably between states

Average Charitable Donation by State (2023–24)

WA

Highest average gift

WA leads, followed by VIC and the ACT. WA's lead is often linked to its high-paying mining workforce, while the ACT's figure reflects a smaller population, which concentrates the average.

THE PEOPLE BEHIND THE NUMBERS

Who pays, and who they are

Behind every aggregate sits a taxpayer: an industry, a residency status, a household.

Average tax paid differs widely by industry

Average Individual Tax Contribution by Industry

5×

Health care versus the lowest

Health care pays about five times the lowest industries, reflecting high-paying professions. Finance and mining follow, while hospitality, transport and administrative support form the lower tail.

Most taxpayers are taxable residents

Number of Individuals by Taxable Status, Residency and Tax Bracket

20%

Of individuals are non-taxable

One in five individuals pays no tax, yet they hold only about 3% of taxable income, so their effect on revenue is small. Non-residents are few, under 2% of individuals, and a small slice of every group.
